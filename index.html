Core lending terms in plain English

  • Business loan: the stated range runs from $10,000 to $5 million, so the amount should follow the commercial purpose rather than the maximum available.
  • Working capital: listed from $20,000 to $500,000+, best separated from expansion funding so the repayment logic stays clear.
  • Equipment finance: borrowing tied to a productive asset such as machinery, plant, medical equipment, vehicles or technology.
  • Security: the asset itself may secure the loan, which changes risk, pricing conversation and end of term options.
  • Pre-approval: stated as often available within 24 hours for standard applications.
  • Full approval: typically described as 2-5 business days, depending on complexity and documentation.
  • Seasonal payments: specifically mentioned for farmers whose revenue moves across the year.
  • Balloon payment: an end of term amount that can lower regular repayments while leaving a final sum to settle or refinance.

Structures named for equipment purchases

Three equipment structures are named: chattel mortgage, finance lease and hire purchase. Under a chattel mortgage, the borrower owns the asset from day one, may claim GST upfront and may benefit from depreciation deductions. Under a finance lease, the lender owns the asset while the borrower leases it, GST is charged on repayments, and the borrower may return or purchase the asset at the end of the term. Hire purchase is named as another available path, with the detail to be confirmed against the asset and accounting advice.

The comparison should not stop at the first repayment. Ownership, GST timing, depreciation, end of term choices and accountant input can change the real cost. A structure that looks cheaper monthly may be less suitable once tax treatment and final ownership are included.

Documents that shape approval speed

Fast timing depends on a complete file. The practical checklist is the asset details, business structure, income evidence, tax position, existing liabilities and supplier invoice. For smaller unsecured loans, same-day approval is described as possible, but it should be treated as scenario dependent rather than guaranteed.

  • Match the invoice or asset quote to the loan purpose.
  • Show how revenue will service repayments, including seasonal gaps.
  • List current liabilities so the lender sees total commitments.
  • Ask what is required for a standard file versus a complex one.
  • Confirm who handles paperwork through to settlement.
